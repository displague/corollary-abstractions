# Roadmap v0.26 — a refusal that says what would have worked, if the course's selection survives adjudication

v0.25 gave the person a way to say what their vocabulary *is*. A declaration
line — a name, an arity, argument categories — reaches a total function that
admits it into a session-scoped ledger or refuses it with **exactly one**
deciding clause, by default toward refusal. Twelve gates green over 14,063
decided inputs, R-H1 licensed, nothing declared surviving the session or
touching the library.

And the cycle's real finding is what the review found underneath that green.
Two of B3's detectors **could not have failed**; B9's registered control
family **could not have fired** on this corpus, whatever the fit half
selected; B3 executes no mutant at all and had never said so; and the served
`declare` grammar example was carrying a sealed **ADMITTED** fixture symbol
in the committed tree — b3-m08's own vector — while B3 scored that mutant
STOPPED. The review found it; no gate did. Run 1 is retained, superseded, and
labelled with what it is and is not evidence of.

Two things v0.25 falsified about its own instruments travel into this one.
First: **a check that cannot go red is not a check**, and the cheapest place
to catch that is at registration — B9's family ceiling was a one-line
computation nobody had done. Second: **a containment gate scoped to a run's
outputs cannot see a value that is already committed**, and a gate whose
mutants are prose has never been run.

This cycle starts from the design those findings forced. As with v0.24 and
v0.25, **this document does not silently promote the incumbent to a scheduled
capability**: §1 is conditional on the roadmap's own adjudication against the
incumbent queue, and STRANGER-GATE's prohibition is a prohibition, not a
candidacy.

## 1. Headline — REPAIRABLE REFUSALS, if the course's selection survives the roadmap's own adjudication

The v0.26 course ran three isolated three-round series — fifteen round-one
directions, **$2.72** total, headless from an empty non-git directory under a
strict tool denylist — and selected **NEAREST YES**
(`reports/design-direction-v0.26.json`), written up as
[DESIGN-repairable-refusal](DESIGN-repairable-refusal.md). The selection was
**cut in half on selection**: the series had elegantly unified declaration
lines with *budgets*, and the budget surface is declined, because shipping it
would unpark the cost lanes by side effect (§3).

**The boundary being moved.** The declaration surface exists precisely so the
person can use vocabulary the program never authored — and when the program
refuses such a line, the clause names **which rule stopped it** and never
**what would have worked**. The author of the corpus can read the clause. A
stranger cannot: there are nine categories, five reserved prefixes and 286
census members they cannot see. The boundary moves when this becomes
recordable: *for a declaration line that reached the grammar's production and
was then refused on a rule, the system either exhibits the least-cost edit
sequence, over an edit algebra frozen as data before the corpus existed, that
the unmodified admitter admits — with exhaustive enumeration of the entire
below-claimed-cost ball as the proof of minimality, re-enumerated by a second
program written against the same frozen specification — or it reports that no
repair exists within the frozen bound, together with the size of the ball it
searched.*

A refusal stops being a wall and becomes an instruction. That is the human
capability: not a better answer, a usable "no."

**The habit it suspends, and the reason this is not an increment.** Every
scored gate this repository has ever run has used **self-authored fixtures**.
H-PRE's fixture seal is the best form of that habit and it is a good
instrument for construction checks; it cannot tell you whether a repair yield
means anything, because the same hand would have written the refusals and the
algebra that repairs them. The scored population here must come from a
**non-author** — the digest-pinned local model `scripts/machine_reader.py`
already carries, drawn through a new free-decode path and sealed. What that
buys is a corpus not authored by the maintainer. What it does **not** buy is
a fact about people: a machine non-author is not a user, and the artifact
header must say so in those words.

**One new artifact kind ships:** `repair_certificate/1`, one record per
refused line, carrying `line_id` and digests but **never the line text** —
corpus lines are sealed values, and a sealed value copied into a committed
artifact is a containment failure a run-scoped sweep cannot see. That is
v0.25's F4 finding turned into a schema decision.

**If the adjudication does not keep it,** this item parks with the design
twice-reviewed and unimplemented, and the incumbent queue is unchanged.

     is therefore the most consequential component in the slice. It reuses
     `machine_reader.MANIFEST` and `verify_weights` for the weights pin but
