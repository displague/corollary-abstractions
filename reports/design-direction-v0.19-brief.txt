You are an outside advisor to a small research program you have never
seen. Everything you know about it is in this message; no code or data
will be provided. Your value here is precisely that you are outside it.

The program's thesis: everything with a closed form belongs in
inspectable code, with learned components carrying only a graded
residual — and never the difference between refusing and answering. It
maintains a graph of about thirteen thousand exact statements across
twenty-seven subject areas: a small hand-authored core with
person-written explanations, and a large machine-ingested layer of
formal mathematical statements that carry no human prose. Everything
regenerates byte-identically from committed seed programs. Structural
ledgers find repeated skeletons across subject areas — the same
equation shape appearing in physics and economics — and an ownership
index answers which statements host a given subterm.

What a person can do today: converse with the graph through a typed
session that answers with receipts, refuses what it cannot ground, and
asks signed clarifying questions when a request is ambiguous — served
both at a terminal and over the standard chat-completions HTTP API
that coding agents attach to. A registered, controlled measurement
this cycle showed the system delivering correct receipt-bearing
answers at hundreds of times the effective throughput of a small
language model that held the very same records in its context window —
the model, even told to quote verbatim, could not reproduce exact
content at speed. A second registered measurement gave the formal
layer a voice: statements render into English sentences gated by
re-parsing each sentence back to the exact structure it claims to
express (99.9 percent round-trip) — but only over the seventeen
percent of the graph whose formal text the program's own internal
grammar can parse; the rest is written in a richer formal dialect the
internal parser does not read. The graph grows at runtime, append-only,
through a proven-write gate: new knowledge is consumed as encountered
and becomes immediately queryable, with no retraining anywhere.

Measurement culture, all binding: preregistration before
implementation; gates frozen before numbers are seen; capability-blind
controls with written voiding sentences; negatives published as
first-class results; receipts kept for failed runs as well as green
ones. Constraints: one maintainer; a first slice must fit one release
cycle and produce a committed, checkable artifact; no multi-hour
training runs; learned components may rank among licensed candidates
but may never author structure or unlock an answer.

The recurring failure mode this program polices in itself: headline
drift toward instruments about its own evidence — meta-work that
audits the project rather than extending what a person can do with it.
The last two cycles were course-corrected by the maintainer toward the
product surface; this inquiry is the outside channel that keeps the
next choice from being an echo.

Propose FIVE distinct forward directions. For each, in under 150
words: (a) the new kind of claim it would make true; (b) why the
current shape cannot make that claim today; (c) a falsifiable
construction gate — what, decided in advance, would count as the idea
failing; (d) the cheapest honest failure. Do not propose: more corpus
entries for their own sake, more twin matches, a model trained to
imitate an exact capability, or another instrument that audits the
program's own evidence trail. Range widely; at least one should feel
risky to you. Number them with short memorable names.
